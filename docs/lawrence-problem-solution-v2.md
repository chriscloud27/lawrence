# Lawrence — Problem, Solution, Agency Value

**Version:** 2.0 — September 2026
**Author:** Chris Weber

---

## The Problem

Parents research schools online, find an agency, and fill out a contact form. Then they wait. The agency receives the form, schedules a discovery call, and spends 20–40 minutes extracting basic information: who is the child, what do they need, what can they afford, when do they need to start.

Only after that call can the agency provide anything of individual value.

This costs both sides:

- **The parent** waits days for a response and gets nothing useful until someone manually processes their case.
- **The agency** burns 60–70% of counsellor time on discovery calls that produce no revenue. 70% of enquiries never even get a response. The 3–5% that convert subsidize the waste.

The contact form collects data but creates no intelligence. The discovery call creates intelligence but doesn't scale. Neither step gives the parent a reason to stay engaged.

---

## The Solution

Lawrence replaces the dead zone between "form submitted" and "first useful conversation" with an AI-driven intake that builds a parent profile before a human is ever involved.

**Step 1 — Structured intake.**
A short form captures the basics: child's age, school type, target geography. This takes under 60 seconds.

**Step 2 — Multi-modal upload.**
The parent is invited to share everything they've already collected: voice notes describing what they want, research documents, school brochures, PDFs, email threads, screenshots of shortlists. Any format. The parent has already done work — Lawrence captures it instead of ignoring it.

**Step 3 — AI profile construction.**
An agent processes every input — transcribes audio, extracts facts from documents, identifies preferences, constraints, and priorities — and assembles a structured parent profile. Budget signals, authority indicators, concrete needs, and timeline pressure are identified automatically.

**Step 4 — Parent review.**
The parent sees their own profile and can correct, add, or refine it. This is the first moment they receive value: their scattered research, validated and organized, reflected back to them. This is the offer they cannot say no to.

**Step 5 — BANT scoring.**
The profile is scored 0–100 across Budget, Authority, Need, and Timeline. The score determines routing:

| Score | Meaning | What happens |
|-------|---------|--------------|
| < 35 | Low fit | Automated resources, no counsellor time spent |
| 35–50 | Unclear fit | AI asks targeted follow-up questions to sharpen the score |
| 50–75 | Qualified | Booking link + email introduction to a counsellor |
| 75–100 | Hot lead | Priority alert, counsellor responds within 2 hours |

**Step 6 — Handoff.**
The counsellor receives a pre-built profile, not a blank contact form. The first human conversation starts at "here's what we recommend" instead of "tell me about your child."

## The Agency Perspective

### What changes on day one

The agency stops doing manual triage. Every enquiry is profiled and scored before a counsellor sees it. Counsellors open their day with a ranked list of parents sorted by fit, not a pile of unread forms.

### The dashboard

A single view shows all active parent profiles with their BANT score, status, and key facts. The agency can:

- **Filter and sort** by score band, school type, geography, timeline.
- **Drill into any parent profile** to see the full AI-constructed summary, uploaded documents, and extracted signals.
- **Add their own notes** — meeting notes, voice memos, follow-up documents — which the agent folds back into the profile to keep it current.
- **Track status** from first contact through to enrollment.

### Pattern intelligence

When 40 parents in a quarter ask about bilingual IB programmes in Switzerland, the agency sees that pattern in aggregate — not buried in individual call notes. These insights feed back into:

- Website content and SEO (write about what parents actually search for).
- Counsellor talking points (lead with what parents care about, not what the agency assumes they care about).
- School partner conversations ("parents are asking for X — do you offer it?").

### What the agency no longer does

- Screen unqualified enquiries manually.
- Spend discovery calls collecting information the parent already has.
- Maintain separate notes across email, spreadsheets, and memory.
- Guess which leads are worth calling back first.

### What the agency gains

- Every counsellor-hour spent on a parent who is likely to convert.
- A system that works across devices, across counsellors, and across time zones.
- An institutional memory that doesn't leave when a counsellor does.
